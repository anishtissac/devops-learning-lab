Understand the idea

Learning target: Understand which devices share a local link and why a router is needed between networks.

A broadcast domain is a local delivery boundary

A VLAN creates a logical link-layer domain on suitable network equipment. Devices in different VLANs do not automatically share the same Ethernet broadcast domain, even if they connect to the same physical switch. A router or another layer-three function is commonly used to move IP traffic between those domains.

Tags describe link-layer membership

An IEEE 802.1Q tag can identify a VLAN on a link carrying multiple VLANs. Access and trunk configuration determine how equipment assigns or carries traffic. An incorrect VLAN can leave a link physically active while placing a host in the wrong local domain. A cable and an UP interface do not prove correct domain membership.

VLAN and subnet are not synonyms

A VLAN is a link-layer segmentation mechanism. An IP subnet is an address-prefix concept. They are often aligned in ordinary designs, but they describe different things. Two addresses with matching prefixes can still fail local delivery if their attachments are separated by the wrong VLAN or isolation rule.

Practical troubleshooting

Evidence that narrows the cause

Check local-domain and VLAN membership as well as IP configuration.

Next action and verification

Is a VLAN the same object as an IP subnet?

Reveal the explanation

No. They belong to different layers, though designs often map one subnet to one VLAN.
