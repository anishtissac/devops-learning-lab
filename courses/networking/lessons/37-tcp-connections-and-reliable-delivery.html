Understand the idea

Learning target: Follow the handshake, sequence numbers, acknowledgements, retransmissions, and connection close.

TCP supplies a reliable ordered byte stream

TCP tracks byte positions and acknowledges received data. Lost data can be retransmitted. The receiver presents ordered bytes while the connection succeeds. TCP does not preserve the boundaries of individual application writes and cannot guarantee that the receiving application committed a business transaction.

The handshake establishes connection state

A common active-open exchange is SYN, SYN + ACK, and ACK. Each side communicates initial sequence information. A completed handshake proves that this setup exchange worked, not that TLS, authentication, or application processing succeeded. SYN consumes one sequence position, which explains why its acknowledgement advances by one in simple examples.

Connections can slow or close in several ways

Flow control helps protect a receiver; congestion control responds to network conditions. Normal close uses FIN-related exchanges, while a reset aborts a connection. Retransmissions are evidence of missing acknowledgements, but the underlying cause can be loss, filtering, overload, or another path problem. Interpret timing and captures together.

Practical troubleshooting

Symptom

TCP establishes, then the client receives an application error.

Evidence that narrows the cause

The setup exchange succeeded; inspect the later protocol and service response.

Next action and verification

Investigate the reported application condition rather than treating every error as a failed handshake.

Retest the original operation from the same source context. A successful test of a different endpoint or protocol does not establish that this symptom is resolved.
