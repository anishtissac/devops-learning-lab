Understand the idea

Learning target: Separate an individual address from the address block it belongs to.

A prefix describes a block

An individual address identifies a particular address value. A network prefix groups addresses by their leading bits. In 192.168.10.25/24, the /24 says that 24 leading bits identify the prefix. The remaining eight bits vary within that block. The address is not converted into a different-sized number when you add the suffix.

The boundary changes interpretation

Two addresses sharing the first three decimal octets are in the same /24 block, but this shortcut does not hold for every prefix. With /25, the final octet is split into two different blocks. With /23, a block spans two values of the third octet. Work from the prefix, not from the appearance of the decimal dots.

Connected reachability is a routing decision

A configured address and prefix commonly contribute a connected route on Linux. That helps the host decide whether a destination belongs on a directly attached network. Routes can express more complicated behaviour, so same-prefix arithmetic is not a complete model of every topology. It is the foundation for understanding why a host might resolve a local neighbour or send toward a gateway.

See the relationships

Work through one example

Compare 192.168.1.10 and 192.168.1.200.

  1. At /24 they share the 192.168.1.0/24 block.
  2. At /25 the first belongs to .0/25 and the second to .128/25.
  3. The two written address values did not change; the boundary used to group them changed.

Practical troubleshooting

Symptom

A host tries neighbour discovery for a destination that should use a gateway.

Evidence that narrows the cause

An overly broad connected prefix can classify the destination as on-link.

Next action and verification

Compare configured prefixes and the selected route with the intended address plan.

Retest the original operation from the same source context. A successful test of a different endpoint or protocol does not establish that this symptom is resolved.
